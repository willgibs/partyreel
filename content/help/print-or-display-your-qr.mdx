A QR code fails for boring reasons: too small, too low-contrast, printed on something shiny, or shown on a screen from across a hall. All of them are easy to avoid.

## Printing the code

<Steps>
<Step title="Download the SVG">
From the share dialog, <UiLabel>SVG (best for print)</UiLabel>. It scales without blur, so one file covers a place card and a banner.
</Step>
<Step title="Size it for the distance">
A rule of thumb: the code should be about a tenth as wide as the distance people will scan it from. A table card scanned from a foot away can be a couple of inches; a sign read from across a room wants to be the size of a dinner plate. Bigger is never a problem.
</Step>
<Step title="Keep the margin and the contrast">
Leave the white border that comes with the file. Print dark on white or cream; don't invert it, tint it, or put it over a photo. Matte paper beats gloss under party lighting.
</Step>
<Step title="Test-scan a proof">
Print one, hold it where it'll live, scan it with your phone camera, and open the link in a private browser window so you see the guest side. Then print the rest.
</Step>
</Steps>

There are no card or poster templates in Partyreel; the SVG drops into any design tool, a word processor, or your invitation printer's upload form.

## On a screen

Use <UiLabel>PNG (best for screens)</UiLabel> for a thumbnail-sized code, and the SVG for anything larger: the PNG is about 200 pixels across and goes soft when a slide blows it up. A few things that matter more than they seem:

- **Plain background, big code.** A slide with the code filling a third of the screen and nothing busy behind it. Avoid animations that move the code.
- **Write the link too.** Some people would rather type than scan. Put the event link in text under the code; a custom link is much friendlier here. See [set a custom event link](/help/custom-event-link).
- **Leave it up.** A code that flashes past in a slideshow loop gets missed. Give it its own slide that stays, or a corner of the screen throughout.
- **Check the distance.** From the back of a room, a phone camera needs a code the size of a laptop screen. Table cards cover the people the screen can't reach.

## Something to say next to it

A short line does the explaining: "Scan to add your photos to our album. No app required." Guests who scan land on a welcome screen that says the same, so you don't need instructions beyond that.

<Callout type="info" title="Want the album on the screen too?">

The same screen can show the album filling up live. See [show the album live on a screen](/help/show-the-album-live-on-a-screen).

</Callout>
