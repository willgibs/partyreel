A QR code is the right tool for a room. For everyone else (the group chat, the family who left early, the people who couldn't come), the link itself is easier.

<Path>Dashboard › Your event › Share</Path>

## Copy and send it

Open <UiLabel>Share</UiLabel> and tap the copy button beside the link. Paste it anywhere: a text, a group chat, an email, the wedding website, the back of the program. It opens the same event page a scan does, and it works before, during, and long after the event.

Guests can pass it on too. The <UiLabel>Invite</UiLabel> button on the event page gives them the code and the link, and the Share action in the photo viewer sends the event link, never a single photo.

## How it looks when you send it

Messaging apps and email clients show a preview card: the event name with a line inviting people to add their photos. A password-protected event shows its name but nothing else; a private event shows a generic card. Nothing in the preview reveals any photo.

## Who can open it

Anyone with the link, subject to your event's visibility: public opens directly, password-protected asks for the password, private shows a friendly locked screen. Search engines are asked not to index event pages, and the address is a long random string, so nobody can find your album by searching or guessing. See [who can see your event](/help/who-can-see-your-event).

## A friendlier address

The permanent link is long. On a paid plan you can add a custom link like `partyreel.com/e/maya-and-sam` that opens the same event and is much easier to say out loud or type under a QR code on a screen. See [set a custom event link](/help/custom-event-link).

<Callout type="tip" title="The link is the album, later">

After the event, the link you sent is the album people come back to. You don't need to send a second "here are the photos" link; see [share the album after the event](/help/share-the-album-after-the-event).

</Callout>
