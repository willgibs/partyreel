You scanned the code, you can see a handful of photos, and the page is asking for your email. That's the host's choice, not a paywall: they've asked that everyone who sees the full album and adds to it confirms who they are, once, with an email.

## What you'll see

The album shows its newest <TeaserCount /> photos as a preview and a button to see the rest. Tap it (or <UiLabel>Continue</UiLabel> on the welcome screen) and the page asks for your email with a short explanation: the host keeps this gallery just for guests, so it's one tap and no password.

<Steps>
<Step title="Enter your email and tap Email me a code">
The page shows exactly which address the code went to.
</Step>
<Step title="Type the 6-digit code from the email">
Or tap the link in the same email; either one works. If it doesn't arrive within a minute, see [the email code didn't arrive](/help/the-email-code-didnt-arrive).
</Step>
<Step title="Add your name">
The first time, Partyreel asks for the name that will appear on your photos. Only hosts ever see your email; guests see this name.
</Step>
</Steps>

You're in: the full album, and <UiLabel>Add photos</UiLabel>.

## That code made you an account

There's no separate sign-up. Confirming your email creates a free Partyreel account, which is what lets you come back to this album later, save it to a dashboard, and keep your uploads under your name across events. Next time, the same email (or <UiLabel>Continue with Google</UiLabel> with the same address) opens the same account. See [save an event, and find your uploads later](/help/save-an-event-and-find-your-uploads).

## Guests never pay

Nothing a guest does costs anything, ever: joining, adding photos, downloading, watching the reel. The host pays for storage if they need more of it. The <UiLabel>Start for free</UiLabel> button in the corner is for people who want to host their own event.

## Rather not?

<UiLabel>Just browsing</UiLabel> keeps the preview open without an account. To add photos, though, this event needs the email. If the host wanted anonymous uploads, the page wouldn't have asked; it's their setting. What they see about you is in [what guests can and can't see](/help/what-guests-can-and-cant-see).

<Callout type="info" title="A password instead?">

Some events ask for a password from the invitation rather than, or before, an email. That's a different gate: see [messages guests might see](/help/messages-guests-might-see).

</Callout>
