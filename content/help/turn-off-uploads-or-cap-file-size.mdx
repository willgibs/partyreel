Two switches shape what guests can add, and neither is tied to a date. You decide when the album is finished, and how big a single upload is allowed to be.

<Path>Dashboard › Your event › Settings › Guest uploads</Path>

## Turn off uploads

Flip <UiLabel>Accepting uploads</UiLabel> off and tap <UiLabel>Save changes</UiLabel>. The upload button disappears for guests, who see <UiLabel>The host has closed uploads. You can still browse the gallery.</UiLabel> Everything else keeps working: viewing, the photo viewer, downloads, the reel. If you've shared a reel, it moves to the top of the page and the album becomes a keepsake. Flip it back on any time.

Your own <UiLabel>Add photos</UiLabel> keeps working while uploads are closed; the switch is for guests.

## Cap the file size

<UiLabel>Max size per upload</UiLabel> limits how large any single guest file can be. Pick a preset from <UiLabel>No limit</UiLabel> (the universal <UploadSize /> ceiling) down to <UploadCapFloor />. A guest whose file is over the cap sees a message naming the limit and nothing uploads.

The cap is for guests only; your own uploads ignore it. It's a way to stop one enthusiastic guest's forty-minute video from eating your storage, not a way to stop uploads (use the switch above for that). The floor is <UploadCapFloor /> so a cap can never accidentally reject every ordinary photo.

## There's no end date

An event is never scheduled to close; the date you enter is a label. On the free plan, an event nobody touches for <InactivityMonths /> is warned by email, then moved to Deleted, where it can be restored for <RecoveryDays />. Signing in is enough to keep it active. Deleting the event yourself is the only other way it ends. See [how long your media is kept](/help/how-long-media-is-kept).

<Callout type="tip" title="When to close">

Not the night of. Give it a few days; the best photos often arrive when people get home and sort their camera rolls. Close it once the trickle stops, so the reel takes its place at the top.

</Callout>
