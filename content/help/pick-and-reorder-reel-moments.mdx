A reel is a list of moments in an order. Everything about editing it comes down to adding, removing, and dragging, and the Studio keeps the reel playing beside you while you do it.

## Three doors in

- **The Moments sheet.** In the Studio, open <UiLabel>Moments</UiLabel>: the whole album as a grid. Tap a photo to add it (it gets a numbered badge), tap again to take it out. Suggested photos carry a soft hint, and <UiLabel>Add suggested</UiLabel> (with the count) takes them all at once. This is the main door.
- **The photo viewer.** Open any photo in the gallery; the host row has the add-to-reel control.
- **Select mode.** <UiLabel>Select</UiLabel> in the gallery, pick a batch, <UiLabel>Add to reel</UiLabel>. See [select many photos at once](/help/bulk-select-and-batch-actions).

There's no add-to-reel chip on the gallery tiles themselves, on purpose; the consequential actions live behind one more tap.

## What qualifies

Only approved photos and videos. Something waiting in Review can't be added until you approve it, and a hidden photo can't be newly added (one that's already in the reel stays, dimmed, until you remove it). Videos join as their opening frame; the reel doesn't play footage.

## Reorder

<Path>Dashboard › Your event › Open studio</Path>

The filmstrip along the bottom of the Studio is the running order. Press and drag a frame to move it; the reel re-cuts as you drop. The trailing plus opens the Moments sheet. Reordering lives here only, so the reel keeps playing while you work.

## The opening shot

The <UiLabel>Cover</UiLabel> sheet picks which moment opens the reel. It's also the poster guests see on the event page before they press play.

## Start over

Take everything out from the Moments sheet and the reel is empty again; there's no separate delete. Or use the fill button on the event page for a fresh first cut. Changing the moments doesn't affect a copy you've already downloaded until you download again.

<Callout type="tip" title="Twelve to twenty">

A <ReelSeconds tier="free" /> reel holds roughly a dozen moments before each one flashes past; a <ReelSeconds tier="pro" /> reel about twice that. The Auto length setting fits the cut to what you picked, so add generously and let it breathe.

</Callout>
