Put the album on a screen in the room and the room starts feeding it. Every photo someone adds shows up for everyone within seconds, which turns out to be all the slideshow you need.

## Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Open the event link in a browser">
On a laptop plugged into the TV or projector, or in the TV's own browser. Use the plain event link (or the custom link), the same one guests get.
</Step>
<Step title="Open it as a guest">
Use a private window, or a device you're not signed in on, so the screen shows the guest view: no stats row, no review queue, no hidden items. The host view works too; it just carries your controls.
</Step>
<Step title="Full-screen it and leave it">
The gallery listens for new uploads and adds them to the top on its own. A tile appears within a few seconds of the upload finishing, with a small settle animation. Nothing to refresh, nothing to click.
</Step>
<Step title="Put the QR code next to it">
On a card in front of the screen, or in a corner of the slide if you're mixing the album with a presentation. The screen makes people want to add; the code lets them. See [print your QR, or put it on a screen](/help/print-or-display-your-qr).
</Step>
</Steps>

## What it looks like

The gallery is a mosaic, newest at the top, so the freshest photos are the ones people see first. Videos show a still frame with a play badge; they don't autoplay. There's no dedicated slideshow or kiosk mode that cycles through photos full-screen, and no way to pin one photo; the live mosaic is the display.

## Keep it presentable

Turn on review if you'd rather see photos before the room does: an approved photo appears on the screen the moment you approve it from your phone. See [review uploads before they appear](/help/review-uploads-before-they-appear). Hiding a photo removes it from the screen just as fast.

If the event asks guests for an email, remember the screen's browser needs to pass that gate once too; a signed-in host's view skips it.

<Callout type="info" title="A long evening on one tab">

The page keeps itself fresh, but a browser left alone for many hours can pause background work. If the screen ever seems stuck, a reload brings it straight back with everything that arrived meanwhile.

</Callout>
