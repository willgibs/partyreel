You wrote a shot list for your photographer. The rings, the first look, the two of you in the doorway with the light doing that thing. They will get all of it, because that is the job and they are good at it.

There is a second list. Nobody writes it down, because nobody can.

## The shot list nobody wrote

Your grandmother's face during the vows, from the third row. The kid under the table with a fistful of cake and no supervision. The aunties' table halfway through the toasts, mid-laugh at something the MC did not say into the microphone. Your cousin who filmed the bouquet toss from the other side of the room, so you can finally see who lunged.

Every one of those photos was taken. They exist right now, on phones, a few feet from each other. Collecting them is not complicated, and [the wedding photo QR guide](/blog/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) walks through the setup end to end. This piece is about why the second list is worth having at all.

## Where those photos go today

Nowhere, mostly. A handful get posted and buried by the next post. A few get sent to the one person who asked nicely. The rest sit in a camera roll between a boarding pass screenshot and a photo of where somebody parked, and within a year they are thousands of photos deep and effectively gone. Nobody is withholding them from you. They simply have no place to land, and "could you send me your photos?" is a favor you have to ask over and over until you stop asking.

## Every guest is a second shooter

Your photographer is one lens moving along one timeline. They are in the right room at the right moment because you paid them to be, and that decision is exactly what makes every other room invisible to them. While the formals happen on the lawn, the cocktail hour is happening without a camera. While the first dance is being covered from the front, nobody is behind you catching your dad's face.

At a wedding, every guest is a second shooter. Not a better one. A differently placed one. The value is not resolution or composition, it is [where they were standing](/features/guests): vantage points you could never have booked, running at the same time, all day, on cameras that were already in the room.

And they see different things, because they know different people. Your college roommate photographs your college friends. Your mother photographs her sisters. The teenagers photograph each other doing something on the dance floor that will be the funniest thing in the album. A hired professional cannot manufacture that, because it is not a matter of skill, it is a matter of belonging to the table.

The gallery is the record of your day. The guest album is the rest of it. Couples who want both should have both, and the photographer or planner can be the one who runs the album. If that is you, [the guest album ships beside your gallery](/blog/guest-album-for-photographers-and-planners).

## What changes when there is one album

One code, one link, one place. That is the whole mechanism, and three things follow from it.

The album fills while the wedding is still happening. Guests scan, upload from the browser, and keep going between courses, so nothing depends on anyone remembering you a week later. There is [no app to install and no password to invent](/help/how-guests-join-and-upload), which is the single thing that decides how many people actually contribute.

What lands is the real file. Not a chat-app copy of a copy, but the photo the phone made, up to <UploadSize /> each. When you download it a year from now, it is still the original.

And you stay in charge of it. Anything can be hidden or removed, and you can hold every upload for approval first if the reception is formal enough to want that. Nothing goes anywhere you did not send it. It is your album before it is anyone else's.

There is a quieter benefit too. Guests can see and download the album, so the person who took a photo of your grandmother laughing gets to keep it as well. Right now that photo is trapped on the phone of whoever shot it, seen by nobody, including them.

## The reel is the ending

Nobody rewatches a whole album. They rewatch one short video. When the album is full, you pick the moments you want and the event ends as a highlight reel you can send to everyone who was there, which is a very different artifact from a folder. [It renders on your own phone](/blog/highlight-reel-renders-on-your-phone), which is why it costs nothing and takes no queue.

## What to do this week

If your wedding is on the calendar, the useful move is small. Decide that the second list is worth collecting, then put one code where guests already sit and tell people it is there. A table card and one sentence during the toasts is all of it. Everything else takes care of itself, because taking photos is the one thing your guests were always going to do anyway.
