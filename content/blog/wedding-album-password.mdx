Almost every couple asks about a password, and almost none of them need one. The instinct is right: a wedding album is personal, and the internet is the internet. The tool is usually wrong.

A password is friction at the door. It is the one protection that costs you uploads, and at a wedding uploads are the entire point.

## What a password actually protects against

Be precise about the threat, because the answer follows from it.

A password protects against someone who has your link but should not see the album. That is a real category, and it has a shape: the link gets forwarded past the guest list, printed on something public, or posted somewhere it should not be. If your code is going on a sign in a hotel lobby that strangers walk through, that is exactly the case a lock is for.

What it does not protect against is your own guests. Everyone at the wedding has the password, because you had to give it to them for the album to work at all. It is not a secret in any meaningful sense once the whole guest list knows it, and the person most likely to forward your album is somebody you invited.

It also does nothing about what is inside the album. That is moderation, not access: hiding, removing, and holding uploads for approval are separate controls, and they are the ones that decide what the album contains.

## The friction it costs

Every step between a scanned code and an uploaded photo loses people. That is the whole mechanic of guest uploads, and it is why the product asks for so little.

A password adds a step at the worst possible moment: a guest is standing at their table, holding a phone in one hand, and now needs a string of characters they were told during a toast they did not fully hear. Some of them will ask the person next to them. Some will decide to do it later, which means never. You will never know which photos you did not get, which is what makes this trade so easy to make badly.

Weigh it honestly. A lock converts a small, unlikely risk into a certain, permanent cost.

## What covers a wedding instead

Two things, and they are already there.

**Keep the link private.** An album opens to whoever has the link, and those links are kept out of search engines, so nobody stumbles onto your wedding by searching for it. The link lives on your table cards and in your group chat. That is a boundary, just a soft one.

**Leave <UiLabel>Require verified emails</UiLabel> on.** It is on by default and free on every plan, and it is the setting doing the real work. A visitor who has not confirmed an email sees only the newest <TeaserCount /> photos and a count of how many more there are, and nothing else reaches their browser. To see the album or add to it, they confirm an email with a one-tap code. So a forwarded link does not hand a stranger your wedding, it hands them a doorway with a name on it, and every upload traces back to a real person.

That combination costs a guest one tap and costs you nothing. [Who can see your event](/help/who-can-see-your-event) covers the settings, and [what happens after a guest scans](/blog/scanned-a-qr-code-where-your-photos-go) shows the same moment from their side.

The third setting is private, which means visible to you and nobody else. That is not a wedding album, it is a vault. Useful when you are still deciding, wrong when the room is waiting.

## When a password is right

There are real cases, and they mostly are not weddings.

A corporate event where the album will carry a client's brand and a sponsor's booth. A venue or a public sign where the code will be seen by people who were not invited. A guest list with a genuine reason for privacy, where the risk of the link traveling outweighs the uploads you will lose. In all three, the lock is buying something specific, and a locked album still shows its name and a count so a visitor knows they are in the right place.
