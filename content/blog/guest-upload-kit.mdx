A QR code that nobody was told about is a piece of decor. The code is the easy half; the sentence somebody says out loud is what decides how full your album gets.

So here is the kit: five pieces of copy you can lift word for word, and an opinion about where the code goes. Everything below assumes the event already exists and the code is printed. If it does not yet, [the wedding guide](/blog/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) and [the party version](/blog/birthday-party-photo-sharing) both cover setup.

## Why the announcement beats the print

Guests are not scanning because they are bored. They scan when three questions get answered in one breath: what is this, what do you want from me, and what do I get.

Most codes answer none of them. They sit on a table card that says "scan me", which reads like a link to a menu, and the guest goes back to their drink. The fix is not a bigger code. It is naming the outcome ("everyone gets all the photos") and removing the fear ("no app, nothing to sign up for") in the same sentence.

Say it once out loud, early, when people are seated and paying attention rather than dancing. One clear announcement is worth more than a code on every surface in the room.

## The five pieces

Copy these as they are, or swap the names. All of them are written to be read aloud without sounding like an ad.

**The table card.** Short enough to read while sitting down, and it names the payoff.

> Add your photos to the album. Point your camera at the code, add whatever you have taken, and everyone goes home with the whole day. No app, no sign-up.

**The MC line.** Timed for a lull, after people have eaten and before the floor fills up.

> Quick one from Sam and Alex before the dancing. There is a QR code on every table for the photo album. Point your phone camera at it, add the photos you have taken so far, and keep adding all evening. Everything lands in one place, and everyone gets the whole set, including the ones you did not see happen. No app to download, nothing to sign up for.

**The slide.** For a screen, a projector, or a laptop by the door. Three lines, big type, the code underneath.

> Add your photos to the album
> Point your camera at the code
> No app, no account, everyone gets everything

**The group-chat message, sent the day before.** This is the highest-yield piece in the kit, because guests arrive already knowing.

> Before Saturday: we made one shared album for everyone's photos. Scan the code on your table when you arrive, or just tap this link. Anything you take goes to the same place, and you can browse and download everybody's photos afterward. [link]

**The thank-you note, sent within a few days.** Sending the album back is what turns a one-off into the thing guests ask you about at the next event.

> Thank you for being there. Here is the album with everyone's photos in it, including all the ones we never saw. Have a look, download whatever you want to keep, and add anything you have not put in yet. [link]

That last instruction matters more than it looks. A good number of photos arrive after the event, from the people who were too busy to upload during it, and nobody adds them unless the note asks.

## Where the code goes

Placement is not about covering the room. Three kinds of place get scanned, and everywhere else is decoration.

**Where people sit.** Table cards, place settings, the menu. This is the workhorse: guests are seated, hands are free, and there is a lull between courses that the code fills better than a phone does.

**Where people queue.** The bar, the buffet, the guest book, the line for the restrooms. A queue is a captive minute with nothing to do in it, and it is where the most unhurried scans happen.

**Where people look up.** The welcome sign, the screen between talks, the back of the program. This one converts worst on its own and best in support of the announcement, because it is what people look at while the MC is speaking.

The rule of thumb: one code where they sit, one where they queue, one where they look up. Any more and it stops registering as an invitation and starts reading as signage. ## Send guests the explainer

The one question you will get is not about the code. It is a version of "what happens to my photos", usually from the guest who is most careful about their phone, and it deserves a real answer rather than a shrug.

There is a post written for exactly that person, from their side of the phone: [you scanned a QR code, here is where your photos go](/blog/scanned-a-qr-code-where-your-photos-go). Send it to anyone who asks, or drop the link in the group chat message above. It answers the account question, the quality question, and the privacy question in one place, so you do not have to.
