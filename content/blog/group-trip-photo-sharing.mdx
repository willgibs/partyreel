Set the album up before the flight, not after the trip. That is the whole trick, and it is the difference between an album that fills itself over a week and a thread of promises to send photos that nobody keeps.

A group trip is the hardest photo problem there is. Six people, six phones, six camera rolls, spread over several days, in places with bad signal, and every one of those phones has the best shot of something the others missed.

## Nobody captures a whole trip

Think about who takes which photo. One person shoots the airport, because they are the one who is early. Somebody else has the only picture of the villa before anyone unpacked. The one good group photo was taken by a stranger on a phone that then went in a pocket for three days. The bride has none of herself.

At the end everyone goes home with a partial version of the same week, and the sharing plan is always the same plan: "I'll send them when I'm back." Some do. They send a bulk album link that expires, or twenty photos into the chat where they immediately get buried, and the rest of the trip stays on the phones that took it.

A single album fixes this by moving the effort to the moment the photo is taken, when it costs nothing, instead of the week after, when it costs attention that everyone has run out of.

## One album, one week, one link

An event is not one afternoon. Create it once and it holds the entire trip, from the first airport selfie to the last sunset, because an event has no end date to work around. Days do not need separating: the album stays in the order things happened, which for a trip is the story anyway.

That has a nice practical effect on signal. Nobody has to upload in the moment. Photos taken on a hike with no bars get added back at the hotel, on the drive, or in the departure lounge, and they slot into the same album as everything else. Yesterday's photos arriving at breakfast is normal, not a failure.

And because the album lives at one link, there is no "who is in the group?" question. Someone joins the trip late, or a partner wants to see it, and they get the link. Nobody is added to a chat.

## During the trip

Say it out loud on the first evening, once, and then never mention it again: everything goes in the album, nobody sends anything to anybody.

The habit that makes it work is uploading in batches, not per photo. Most people end up doing it at some dead moment: the taxi, the queue, the hour before dinner when everyone is on their phone anyway. Two or three of those over a week is the entire contribution, and by day three the album has become the thing people scroll instead of the group chat, because it is where the good photos are.

Watch for the two shots that always go missing. The one of the person who takes all the photos, and the group photo at the very start when everyone still looks rested. Get those in early.

## When you get home

You already have the album, so the after-trip work is the fun part rather than the admin part.

- **Everyone takes the whole week home.** Any member of the group can download the full album at original quality, including the shots from the four phones that were not theirs.
- **The reel is the thank-you note.** The album already plays the whole week back as a highlight reel; cut a short clip of it and send it to the group while everyone is still in the post-trip slump. [How a clip gets made on your phone](/blog/highlight-reel-renders-on-your-phone) explains what that involves.
- **The album stays.** Same link next year, when someone wants the photo of the sunset for the anniversary of the trip. Nothing expires underneath it.

If this went well, the same setup works for the family version of the trip ([one album for every generation](/blog/family-reunion-photo-sharing)) and for the party at the end of it ([collecting every birthday photo](/blog/birthday-party-photo-sharing)).
