Three weeks after the offsite, somebody in a Monday meeting says "wasn't there a photo of the whiteboard from day two?" There was. It is on a phone belonging to a person who left the company in the meantime, or it is in a thread nobody can search, or it is on the camera roll of someone who meant to share it and then spent the trip home in an airport queue. The retreat happened. The record of it did not.

That gap is worth closing, and closing it takes one code and about a minute of setup.

## The offsite's photo problem

An offsite is the one work event where people take photos for their own reasons. Not content, not coverage: the team at the trailhead, the person who fell asleep on the bus, the whiteboard that turned out to matter. Those are the photos that make an all-hands land and that new hires get shown a year later.

They are also the ones with no home. The company account has the posed group shot, and everything else scatters. Nobody is going to run a collection drive over chat, and if they do, they will end up with a folder of screenshots at a quarter of the original size.

One album fixes the distribution problem, not the photography problem. Everybody is already taking the pictures. The only thing missing is one obvious place to put them.

## The first slide

Open the offsite the way you open any session, with a slide. Put the code on it, say the line out loud once, and leave the slide up while people find seats.

That single moment does most of the work, because it is the one time in the whole retreat when every attendee is in one room, looking in one direction, with a phone in reach. If you wait until the evening to mention it, you have already lost the travel photos and the morning session. The [guest upload kit](/blog/guest-upload-kit) has the line to read and the slide text to copy.

After that, the code wants a second home. A printed card on the dinner tables works, and so does the code pasted in the offsite channel where people will look for the agenda anyway.

## Verified emails and review, the office defaults

Two settings cover an internal event, and both defaults are already right.

Leave <UiLabel>Require verified emails</UiLabel> on. Colleagues have work email, the confirmation is a one-tap code rather than a password, and it means every upload carries a confirmed name. That matters more than it sounds: when a photo goes into a deck or a hiring page later, you can tell who took it.

Review is the second call, and it depends on your leadership. If somebody senior wants a look before anything is visible to the wider company, turn review on and clear the queue at the end of each day with <UiLabel>Approve all</UiLabel>, pulling the two or three that need a second thought. If your culture is looser, leave it off and let the album fill live while people watch it on the screen in the main room. [Curating your album](/help/moderate-and-curate-your-album) covers both.

<Callout type="tip" title="Say the boring part out loud">

One sentence at the start prevents every awkward conversation later: photos land in a shared work album, anyone at the company can see it, and if you would rather something came down, ask and it comes down. People self-moderate well when they know the rules.

</Callout>

## During: the hike, the dinner, the team-building thing

The album fills without anyone managing it, which is the point, but there are three moments worth nudging.

The travel day. People take their funniest photos in transit, and they take them before anyone has mentioned the album. Post the link in the offsite channel the morning everyone flies.

The activity. Whatever the middle of your retreat is, a hike, a cooking thing, a session that runs long on a whiteboard, that is where the photos with faces in them come from. Ask one person on each team to be the one who posts theirs before dinner, and the rest follow.

The dinner and the evening after it. This is where the album earns the retreat. Put it on the screen in the room and it turns into ambient entertainment, filling in real time as people upload from the table. Long videos are fine here: a file can run up to <UploadSize />, so the toast and the terrible karaoke both fit, on a plan that includes video.

## The all-hands closer

Plan for the all-hands two weeks out. Two weeks after the offsite, at the next all-hands, you open with the album's highlight reel on the big screen. Not a produced video, not an edit somebody stayed late for: the reel the album makes of itself, or a clip you make of it on your phone that morning.

It works because it is not a marketing artifact. It is the team, at the trailhead, at dinner, in the session that ran long, looping on the screen while people find their seats. The people who were there recognize themselves, and the people who were not get the closest thing to having been. [How the reel and its clips work](/blog/highlight-reel-renders-on-your-phone) explains why both run on your own device rather than in a queue somewhere.

Send the album link in the same follow-up email. For the internal newsletter or the careers page, pull a zip of the originals instead of screenshotting the album.

## The archive

The album stays at one link after the retreat ends. That link is the useful thing six months later, when somebody wants the photo of the whiteboard, or a recruiter wants a real picture of the team, or you are building next year's offsite proposal and need to show the last one.

It is also worth reusing the pattern. Whatever the next company thing is, the flow is identical: create the event, put the code on the first slide, say the line.
