Your couple will ask for the guest photos. Not at delivery, but three weeks later, when an aunt mentions a shot from the cocktail hour that nobody has seen. And you will do what everyone does: forward the request into a group chat and hope.

There is a better version of that, and it belongs to you rather than to them.

## What the guest album adds to your delivery

Your gallery is the record of the day, made deliberately, by someone who was hired to be in the right room at the right moment. It is not supposed to contain the getting-ready hour you were not in, the table you did not shoot, or the after-party you had already left.

A guest album is that second layer. It runs on one QR code, fills from the room's phones during the day, and lands next to your gallery as a different kind of object: unposed, everywhere at once, and complete in exactly the ways a single photographer cannot be. [What your couple's guests are shooting all day](/blog/wedding-photos-photographer-cant-be-there-for) is the case for why it matters.

For you, it does something more practical. It gives you a place to send every "do you have a photo of..." request for the rest of the year, and it is a line on your package that a couple understands immediately.

## You are the host

This is the part that matters operationally: the album is yours, not the couple's.

You create the event under your own account. You style the QR code to your suite, so the card on the table looks like it came from your studio rather than from a software company. You hand the couple the link, and they hand it to their guests. When the album fills, you [decide what stays in it](/features/curation): hold uploads for approval if the wedding is formal, hide the accidental shots of the floor, and the album your client sees is the album you curated.

There is no co-hosting and no shared login. One account owns the event, which is a cleaner arrangement than it sounds: your client never has to manage a setting, and you never have to explain one. They get a link that works.

Then you deliver twice. The gallery, on your normal schedule. The album, with a zip of the originals and a highlight reel cut from what the guests captured, which arrives while the wedding is still the thing everybody is talking about. [The reel renders on a device rather than in a queue](/blog/highlight-reel-renders-on-your-phone), so there is no rendering fee to pass on and nothing to wait for.

## What it is not

Worth being blunt, because the boundary is the reason to trust the rest.

This is not a client gallery. There is no proofing, no favoriting workflow for a couple to hand back, no RAW ingest, no print store, no contract or invoice attached. It does not deliver your work, and it should not: your gallery does that job better than a guest tool ever will.

It is also not professional coverage. Guest photos are guest photos, taken on phones, at eye level, with the flash on. They are valuable because of where they were taken, not how.

Sell it as what it is. A complete, curated collection of the day from everyone who was there, delivered by you, alongside the work you were hired to do.
