Price a QR photo app for a user conference and you learn something uncomfortable: the quote goes up with the guest list. Twelve hundred attendees, so a bigger tier. The satellite dinner adds a hundred more, so another tier. The event that would benefit most from collecting everyone's photos is the one the pricing model taxes hardest, and the usual response is to buy a smaller plan and tell fewer people about the code.

That is a strange thing to optimize. You spent a year getting people into the room. Rationing who is allowed to contribute is the last thing the budget should be doing.

## The invoice that grows with the guest list

Apps that charge per guest are pricing a number that has nothing to do with what they store or serve. Their bill tracks your attendance, so every decision downstream gets warped by it: the code goes on the sponsor booth but not the badge, the satellite events get left out, and somebody spends an afternoon guessing at a headcount for a purchase order.

It also makes the thing impossible to budget. Registration moves right up to the doors. Walk-ups happen. A tier you sized in March is the wrong tier in September, and finding out costs money at exactly the moment you have no attention to spare.

## What costs money is bytes, not people

Here is the mechanism, which is the only honest reason a price should exist. A photo occupies space and gets served when someone opens it. That cost is real and it scales with the media, not with the person who took it. A guest who scans your code and uploads nothing costs nothing to have around. A thousand of them cost nothing to have around.

So Partyreel is priced by storage. There is no guest limit and no per-guest fee on any plan: a thousand guests cost the same as ten. Every scan, every upload, every download and every share is included, which means the only question you have to answer before an event is how much media it will produce. [How much storage event photos need](/blog/how-much-storage-for-event-photos) walks through that estimate for real events.

## What you give up

Attendance costs you nothing, so there is nothing to give up there: no cap, no overage, no upgrade prompt at the door. The honest limits are elsewhere.

The free tier is photos only, with <EventLimit tier="free" /> event, which makes it a fine way to try the flow on a small internal event and the wrong choice for a conference where you want the keynote videos. Video starts with the Event Pass. And storage is a real ceiling rather than a soft one: if you collect more than your plan holds, you buy more room or you clear space, which is the trade that lets the guest list be free. ## The sponsor question, in one line

Sponsors usually want two things from event photos: their activation in the recap, and confidence that nothing embarrassing is going out under the event's name. Both are settings rather than plan tiers, and [conference photo sharing without an app](/blog/conference-photo-sharing-no-app) covers how to set them.

If you are sizing a plan this week, start from the media, not the manifest. Estimate the photos, pick the storage that holds them, and then tell every single person in the room to scan.
