You wake up, make coffee, and open the chat. Forty-seven messages since you went to bed.

Somewhere in there is the photo of everyone around the cake with your grandmother mid-laugh, which you know exists because you watched three people take it. What is in the thread is a version of it somebody screenshotted out of a different thread, a video that plays at the size of a postage stamp, four voice notes, a long argument about who left a jacket, and two people typing "can you send me the one of me and Dev". Nobody sends it. The thread moves on. By Wednesday the whole thing has scrolled past the point anyone will go back to.

That is the party. That is where it went.

## Why the chat cannot do this job

A group chat is a brilliant thing that was never built to be an archive, and every one of its virtues works against you here.

It is a **stream**, so the newest thing is the only visible thing, and photos slide out of reach in the order they were sent rather than the order anything happened. It is **many streams**, since half the room is in the main group, a few are in a smaller group, three are in your direct messages, and someone posted the good ones to a story that expired. It sends **copies, not files**, so what arrives has been resized for a screen and what gets forwarded is resized again ([the compression chain](/blog/does-whatsapp-compress-photos) is uglier than most people realize). And it is **conversation**, so photos are interleaved with jokes and logistics forever, which is charming today and unsearchable in a year.

Add those up and you get the real problem. There is no moment at which the party's photos exist as a set. They exist as a hundred fragments in a dozen places, each one held by a different person who thinks somebody else has the good version.

## What "collected" would mean

Try a test. A month from now, could you hand your grandmother every photo taken of her at that party, in one go, at the size it was shot?

If the answer is a chat, the answer is no. It is not a question of effort. You could spend a Saturday scrolling and saving, and you would still miss the photos that never got posted at all, which is most of them. The camera roll of the friend who took the best shots and forgot to share is not somewhere your Saturday can reach.

Collected means four small things: one place, everyone can add to it, everyone can take from it, and nothing was squeezed on the way in. A chat gives you the second one and calls it a day.

## One code instead

Here is the whole alternative, and it is deliberately unglamorous. Before the party you make an event and get one QR code. You put it on the table where the food is and text the link to the group once. Guests point their camera at it and upload from the browser: no app store, no password, no new group to join.

Then three things happen that a thread cannot do. The album fills **while the party is still going**, so the collecting is finished by the time people are looking for their coats. Everyone who has the link can **browse the whole thing and download whatever they want**, so "can you send me the one of..." stops being a favor somebody has to remember. And you keep the album at one link afterward, so the photos have an address instead of a position in a scroll.

The chat still exists, by the way. It is still where the jokes go, still where somebody posts the photo of the jacket. It is just no longer carrying a job it was never built for. If your party is a birthday, [the party playbook](/blog/birthday-party-photo-sharing) has the specifics; if you want to see how a chat scores against every other option side by side, [the ranking](/blog/best-way-to-share-event-photos) does exactly that.

## The next party

You cannot rescue the last one. The photos from that party are where they are, and the honest advice is to ask the two or three people who took the most to upload from their camera rolls into whatever you set up next, because those originals are still sitting on their phones.

The next one is easy to get right, and the deciding happens before anyone arrives, not after. Make the album first, put the code where people put their drinks, and let the room fill it. [Create your first event](/help/create-your-first-event) takes about a minute, and if you want the reasons behind it, [what a shared album does for a party](/events/parties) is the long version.
