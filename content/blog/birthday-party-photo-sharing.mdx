Think about the last birthday party you threw. You have maybe fifteen photos from it, all taken from wherever you happened to be standing, and you are in three of them. Everyone else in the room has fifteen photos too, from angles you never saw, and those photos are going to stay exactly where they are unless somebody makes it effortless to hand them over.

A code by the cake is the whole intervention. Here is how to run it so the album is full before the last guest finds their coat.

## Why party photos vanish

Nobody withholds party photos on purpose. They just never get around to it. The photos sit in a camera roll behind four hundred other photos, and the effort of picking, sending and captioning them is more than the moment seems to warrant a week later.

The other half of it is that there is no obvious destination. Send them where? To the host, individually? To a chat where half the guests are missing? Given no place to put something, people put it nowhere. A shared album with a code on the table removes the decision entirely: there is one place, it takes one tap, and it is right there while the party is happening.

## Set it up in a minute

Create the event, name it after the birthday, and you are done. The only screen that matters is the one with your QR code and link on it, and [create your first event](/help/create-your-first-event) walks it end to end.

Do this the day before, not while guests are arriving. Print a couple of cards, and put the link in whatever chat you used to invite people, so the ones who prefer tapping a link never have to hold a phone up to a table card at all.

## The one setting per party

There is one real decision, and it depends on who is in the room.

Every new event starts with <UiLabel>Require accounts to upload</UiLabel> on. A guest scans, confirms an email with a one-tap code, and uploads. No password, no app, no form. In exchange, every photo arrives with a name on it, which is what you want at an adult party where you might want to thank someone for a shot later.

Turn it off for a kids' party. At a six-year-old's birthday, the uploaders are other parents standing in a doorway holding a coat and a party bag, and asking them for an email address is one thing too many. With the requirement off, anyone with the code uploads instantly and anonymously. You will lose the names, and you will gain about twice the photos. [How guests join and upload](/help/how-guests-join-and-upload) covers both paths.

<Callout type="tip" title="Match the setting to the doorway test">

Picture the least motivated adult at your party, holding a drink, standing up. If the answer to "would they finish this?" is no, switch the requirement off for that event. You can turn it back on afterwards.

</Callout>

## During the party

Two things to do while it is happening, and neither takes attention away from your own party.

Put the code where people already stop. The cake table is the single best spot, because everyone gathers there, everyone has a phone out for the candles, and the queue for a slice is dead time. A second card by the drinks does the rest.

Then put the album on a screen. Open it on the TV and leave it: new uploads appear as they arrive, so the room ends up watching itself, and the guests who have not scanned yet ask what it is. That is the moment the album goes from a dozen photos to a hundred.

And take the group photo with the code visible somewhere. There is a specific reward here: with everyone else uploading, you finally get a group photo that includes whoever normally takes it. The person who has spent every party behind a camera is in this one.

## After the cake

Give it until the next morning, because the best late uploads come from people going through their camera rolls on the sofa.

Then <UiLabel>Download all</UiLabel> for a zip of the originals: full-quality files, not the shrunken copies a chat would have handed you. Hide anything nobody needs, keep the link live, and send it to everyone who came. If you want the short version, the album can end as a highlight reel you send to the group.

If you want video, the speeches, the singing, the kid blowing out the candles in slow motion, that comes with the Event Pass or Pro.
