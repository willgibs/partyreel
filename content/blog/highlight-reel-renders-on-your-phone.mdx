Nobody forwards an album. They forward a video. For years that meant somebody had to sit down the morning after, pick the shots, pick a song, and export the thing before anyone lost interest. Here nobody does that. The album plays itself as a highlight reel while the party is still going, and the piece you send is a clip you make on your own phone.

## The album is the whole event, the reel is the album moving

By the end of the party your guests have filled the album with more than anyone will ever scroll: every angle of the toast, the shots [your photographer can't be there for](/blog/wedding-photos-photographer-cant-be-there-for), a dozen versions of the same group photo where one person blinked in each. That pile is the record, and you want all of it at full quality, forever.

The reel is that record in motion. From the album's second photo it plays as a looping montage of everything guests can see, mixed afresh each time round so nobody's photos bunch together and the newest arrive soon. An upload joins it the moment it lands. A photo the host hides leaves it just as fast. There is no first cut to make, no draft to publish and no file to wait for, which means there is nothing for the host to forget to do.

## It plays at the party, not the morning after

A reel that exists from the second photo changes when people watch it. It plays at the top of the album on every guest's phone, so the first thing someone sees after adding a photo is the party playing back with theirs in it. And it plays on the wall: the host opens the reel on the laptop plugged into the TV, presses Play on a screen, and the room watches itself fill, with the event's code in the corner of the frame for anyone who hasn't added theirs yet.

That screen is the best argument for adding a photo anyone at a party will ever hear. People see a friend's shot appear, reach for their phones, and a minute later they are the ones on the wall.

## The part you send is yours to make

The reel belongs to the event. A clip belongs to you. Anyone who can open the album can tap Make your own on the reel and cut a short video of their own: it starts from the reel's picks, and from there you choose the moments, one of <ReelStyleCount /> looks, a length, and portrait or landscape. Share it straight to the group chat or your story, or save it to your camera roll. On a paid event you can also add it to the album, where it sits beside the photos as an ordinary video, though the live reel never plays a clip back to itself.

The click-by-click version lives in the help center: [make your own clip](/help/make-your-own-clip).

## Why all of it runs on your phone

Most tools that turn your media into a video upload it to a machine somewhere, put you in line behind everyone else who pressed the button, and send you a link when it is your turn. That queue is why those tools charge per render, or ration them, or make you wait.

Nothing here works that way. The reel is drawn on each viewer's own device, straight from the album's pictures, so there is no video to store and no link to expire. A clip is encoded in your own browser by the same drawing code the reel uses:

- **No queue.** The only machine involved is the one in your hand.
- **No fee, on any plan.** There is no render bill to pass on, so the reel and a clip cost nothing extra, including on Free.
- **The file is yours first.** A clip lands on your device the moment it is made. Nothing is uploaded anywhere unless you add it to the album yourself.

<Callout type="warning" title="The honest limit">

Making a clip on the device means the device has to be able to encode video. A modern phone or laptop browser can. An older one cannot, and rather than hand you a button that fails, Make your own shows greyed out and says why when you tap it. The reel still plays either way.

</Callout>

## How long a clip runs, and the mark on a free one

A Free event's clips run up to <ReelSeconds tier="free" /> seconds and carry a small partyreel.com mark in the corner. An Event Pass or Pro event's clips run up to <ReelSeconds tier="pro" /> seconds with no mark at all. The reel itself carries no mark and has no length on any plan, and the clip maker says plainly which events mark their clips, so nobody mistakes the mark for part of every clip.

That is the whole difference. The looks, the moments, the portrait and landscape switch, the sharing and saving: identical on every plan. Length is not a cost being passed on, because a clip is free to make at any scale. It is a line, drawn on purpose, and it is better said plainly than dressed up as a technical limit.

And because [the album has no expiry date](/blog/event-album-no-expiry-date), the reel keeps playing at the same link for as long as you keep the event, ready for the next person who wants a clip of it. Watch it run before you host anything: the [reel page](/reel) plays the engine live.
