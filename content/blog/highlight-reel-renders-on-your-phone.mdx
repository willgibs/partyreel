Nobody forwards an album. They forward a video. The album is where the whole event lives, and the reel is the one piece of it that travels: a short cut with a first frame and a last frame, made to be watched once and passed on. Every event should end with one, and yours gets made on the phone in your hand rather than in somebody's render queue.

## The album is the whole event, the reel is the part you send

By the end of the party your guests have filled the album with more than anyone will ever scroll: every angle of the toast, the shots [your photographer can't be there for](/blog/wedding-photos-photographer-cant-be-there-for), a dozen versions of the same group photo where one person blinked in each. That pile is the record. You want all of it, and you want it at full quality, and you want it forever.

But the record is not the thing you send to twelve people the next morning. That thing has to be short, it has to open well, and it has to end. So the reel is not a second product bolted onto the album. It is a selection from it. You are not making a video. You are choosing from one you already have.

## What making one asks of you

The first cut is built for you out of what is already in the album, and it plays immediately. From there everything happens in one room. You pick the moments, drag them into the order you want while the reel keeps playing beside you, choose a look from <ReelStyleCount /> styles, and set portrait or landscape depending on where it is going to be watched.

Nothing encodes while you do any of that. The preview is a live player drawing exactly the frames the finished file will contain, so what you are watching is the video itself, not a rough idea of it. The click-by-click version lives in the help center: [the highlight reel](/help/the-highlight-reel).

## Why it renders on your phone

Most tools that turn your media into a video upload it to a machine somewhere, put you in line behind everyone else who pressed the button, and mail you a link when it is your turn. That queue is why those tools charge per render, or ration them, or make you wait.

Yours does not work that way. The encode runs in your own browser, on your own device, from the same drawing code the preview used. That single decision explains most of what the reel feels like:

- **No queue.** There is nobody ahead of you, because the only machine involved is yours.
- **No fee, on any plan.** There is no render bill to pass on, so a reel costs nothing extra, including on Free.
- **The file is yours first.** It saves to your device the moment the encode lands, before anything is uploaded anywhere. A flaky hotel connection cannot take it away from you.

<Callout type="warning" title="The honest limit">

Encoding on the device means the device has to be able to encode. A modern phone or laptop browser can. An older one cannot, and rather than hand you a button that fails, the download is replaced with a plain notice. The reel still plays and shares either way.

</Callout>

## How long it runs, and the mark on the free one

A Free event makes a <ReelSeconds tier="free" /> second reel carrying a small partyreel.com mark in the corner. An Event Pass or Pro event makes a <ReelSeconds tier="pro" /> second reel with no mark on it at all.

That is the whole difference. The styles, the studio, the reordering, the portrait and landscape switch, the download: all identical on every plan. Length is not a cost being passed on, because the render is free at any scale. It is a line, drawn on purpose, and it is better said plainly than dressed up as a technical limit.

## A video gives you a frame, not a clip

Here is the part worth knowing before you plan around it. When a video from the album goes into the reel, it contributes its poster frame. It appears as a still image, cut and graded like every other shot.

The clip itself is untouched. It sits in the album at full quality, plays in the browser, and downloads with everything else. So if the moment that matters is a speech, its home is the album, and the reel gets the face in the crowd watching it.

## Share it when it is ready

Your guests see none of this while you work. The reel appears on the event link only when you share it, and from that moment anyone holding the link can watch it and keep their own copy.

That timing is what makes the reel a good closing move. The album filled itself live during the event; the reel arrives a day or two later, at the same link everyone already has, with no new thing for anyone to open. And because [the album has no expiry date](/blog/event-album-no-expiry-date), the two of them stay parked there together for as long as you want them.
