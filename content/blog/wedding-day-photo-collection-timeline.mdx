Guest photos are not a task, they are a schedule. Every one of them is taken at a specific moment by somebody who is holding a drink, and whether it reaches you depends almost entirely on what was in front of them at that moment.

So here is the day as a clock. The setup itself lives in [the wedding photo QR guide](/blog/qr-code-for-wedding-photos); this is when to do what.

## Four weeks out: the decisions

Create the event and get the code, because everything downstream needs it to exist. Then make two calls while you still have time to change your mind.

First, whether guests confirm an email before uploading. On means every upload has a name behind it. Off means a guest can upload without typing anything at all. Most weddings are happier with it on, and it is free either way.

Second, review or live. Review holds uploads for your approval; live lets the album fill in front of everyone. A formal reception usually wants review. A backyard wedding usually does not. [Curate what shows up in your album](/help/moderate-and-curate-your-album) covers switching either way, including mid-event.

Then order the cards. Printing is the only part of this with a lead time, and it is the part people leave until the week of.

## One week out: the test scan and the heads-up

Scan your own printed card, with your own phone, standing in bad light. It takes a moment and catches the one failure that ruins everything else: a beautiful card nobody can read.

Then tell people it exists. A code discovered at the table gets scanned by the curious; a code people were told about in advance gets scanned by everyone. One message to the group chat, one line to the wedding party, one note to whoever is running the microphone. The [guest upload kit](/blog/guest-upload-kit) has copy you can paste for all three.

## The day itself

### The morning

Open uploads before hair and makeup start. The getting-ready hours produce some of the loosest, best photos of the whole wedding, and they happen in two rooms your photographer has to choose between. Drop the link into the wedding party's chat and let it run.

### The ceremony

Phones away, and that is fine. You do not want a row of raised screens between you and the aisle, and the album does not need them. Your photographer owns this half hour. Everything before and after is where guests earn their keep.

### The cocktail hour

The single highest-yield window on the clock. People are standing up, talking to people they have not seen in years, holding a drink in one hand and a phone in the other. Put a code where they queue: the bar, the guestbook table, wherever the line forms. Scans happen in the gaps, and the gaps are here.

### The reception

The code is already on the tables, so the reception's real job is the reminder. One line from whoever has the microphone, delivered between the toasts and the dancing, is worth more than any signage. If there is a screen, put the album on it and let the room watch it fill: nothing recruits uploaders like seeing your own photo appear.

### The last hour

Ask once more, near the door, when people are saying goodbye. The after-party shots and the send-off arrive on the way home, in the car, from the hotel lobby. The album stays open, so late uploads land exactly where the early ones did.

## The morning after

Coffee, then three passes.

Approve, if you ran review: the queue clears in one scroll, and <UiLabel>Approve all</UiLabel> takes the whole thing at once. Then curate, which mostly means hiding the duplicates and anything blurry enough to be a mystery. Then <UiLabel>Download all</UiLabel>, which packages the originals into one zip so the whole day also lives on a drive of your own.

Expect a second wave over the next week, when people go back through their camera rolls and find the good one. Leave uploads open for it. When the flow stops, the album is the whole day, and it can end as [a highlight reel that renders on your own phone](/blog/highlight-reel-renders-on-your-phone).

## The whole clock, in one block

- **Four weeks out**: create the event, decide accounts and review, order the cards.
- **One week out**: test-scan the printed card, tell the group chat, brief the microphone.
- **Morning of**: open uploads, link in the wedding party's chat.
- **Ceremony**: phones away, no action needed.
- **Cocktail hour**: a code where the line forms.
- **Reception**: one spoken reminder, album on the screen.
- **Last hour**: one last ask by the door.
- **Morning after**: approve, curate, download, then leave it open a week.

Print this, hand it to whoever is helping, and go get married. If you have not set the event up yet, start with [the wedding photo QR guide](/blog/qr-code-for-wedding-photos) or see what [one album for the whole wedding](/events/weddings) collects.
