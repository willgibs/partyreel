## Questions (a recommended answer each; the Orchestrator relays them)

Each recommended answer is BUILT and his to overrule; none is a one-way door.

- **Which shape stops the bar wearing a trail its route never set?** Built: the trail is the mounted `SetCrumbs`'s (released when it unmounts, the incoming route's claim replacing it in the same commit), a route's skeleton holds the last trail through its wait (`RouteSkeleton` wears `CrumbsHold`), and a trail nobody claims or holds is dropped. Neither of the brief's two shapes as written: measured on the real router over stand-in routes shaped like `(app)`, a route with a `loading.tsx` commits its NEW address with the skeleton on screen and the page lands a wait later (400 ms in the harness), so a bar tied to the path blinks empty for the whole wait on every hub -> room step (the comment's blank frame, at scale); and every route setting its own is eight files (the trail-less pages, `not-found`, `error`) that goes stale again through any branch that forgets. The failure is safe both ways here: a new `loading.tsx` that forgets its hold only blinks (a scan holds every `loading.tsx` to `RouteSkeleton`), and no route can inherit a trail it did not set. The overrule: the path-tied bar (simpler, blinks) or one `<SetCrumbs trail={[]}>` per route.
- **What does the article's first screen picture?** Built: the photo viewer with the flag picked out (the step is "Tap Report"), the photo's form on the second (the row that names the one photograph, a kind picked, the Reason note, the email block the old picture had dropped), the toast over the viewer on the third; step 1's sentence names the flag and where it sits (the old words said "beside Save and Share": Copy link is between them). The overrule: the form freshly opened on the first, the old pairing, which pictures the form twice and the flag only in passing.
- **Does the one helper carry the popup's differences as an option?** Built: yes, `many` (a marker is ours only by its id, an entry a refresh stripped only at its address): the popup's own; the hub's and the reel's are the default. The overrule: leave the popup on its own (it never restamps and keeps its own lifecycle).
- **The floor after which a Back that never lands lets go:** 1000 ms (a person's double tap lands inside a few ms of the first popstate; the floor only has to outlast a slow phone). His to move.
