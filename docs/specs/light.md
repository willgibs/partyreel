> **NOT LAW** until Will rules; the board is `/design/lab/light` while it stands, and every paste it
> offers is generated by the board's own code, never typed by hand. The record of each round is the

## What is ruled

Will, round five (2026-09-16):

- **The kit lands as written.** Light sorts by what it is doing (separating, filling a room, marking a
  moment) rather than by where it comes from, and a lamp needs a PLACE (an edge, a boundary, a screen,
  a plate) rather than an object throwing it.
- **The shadows go first**, before any coloured phase.
- **Accent is the global register** for the field ("identity feels way too weak").

Will, round seven (2026-09-17):

- **The Aurora is one light in three kept forms:** the seam, the throw and the field. It shipped the
  same day (`SectionLight`, the Library's Aurora entry).
- **Never on a light ground** ("No light ground usage is a decision for now"), enforced in
  `globals.css` rather than by a prop.
- **The clock is 8 seconds** for every lamp, the footer included; the field runs three laps of it.
- **The publish flourish is the house five**, or sampled where the light bleeds from media ("Don't
  need a single stray glow color").
- **What lands second is the home page's two ends.**

## What is open: six steps, in the order the wiring needs them

Round eight is not a new exploration. Round seven's six returned cards all said the card did not show
what it was asking, so each step is a question in plain words, its options as tiles on ONE specimen at
true pixels, and a live stage under them.

1. **Where the Aurora sits** on a section with no photos: both edges, the top, the bottom, or the whole
   section. Drawn with the shipped `SectionLight` on the home page's closer. The home page's two call
   sites wait on this answer and on nothing else, which is why it opens the walk.
2. **Whether dark mode gets shadows:** none, under cards and under menus, or under menus only. One dark
   scene holds all four depth techniques at once, and a legend beside it says how they work together:
   the lighter panel and the thin outline ship everywhere and are not asked; the small shadow (one card
   on another) and the larger one (a layer over the page) are the question. Both shadows fall on the
   same photograph, and the front card sits ABOVE the one behind it, because a shadow falls down. Light
   mode keeps today's bytes and is not drawn.
3. **The thin bright edge** on photos, players and the QR card: one large photograph as today and with
   it, its top left corner enlarged four times on both, then the three kinds of surface it would land
   on. The board recommends skipping it and says why on the step.
4. **The streak of light** over a photo as it lands: the glow's comet and edge ring, drawn over the
   photo and played once. It needs a play-once mode the engine does not have.
5. **The glow that stays** after a publish: the same swell in the house five, resting at a soft light
   or returning to nothing. The QR card already ships the resting form.
6. **The glow behind a button:** one dark button a page, or none.

Three of round seven's twelve were never decisions and have left the walk: the lighter panel and the
thin outline ship on every surface, and the beam ships on the Pro card and is ruled.

## What round eight found, for whoever wires any of this

- **A light that lands ON an object is drawn OVER it.** Round seven mounted the sweep behind an opaque
  frame the same size as its own box, so the band travelled where nobody could see it, and wrapped a
  real `Button` around the halo's wash, whose fill is opaque. Over media the light also wants an
  additive blend, or it reads as haze.
- **The engine re-keys only a bloom on `runId`.** A replay of any other shape is a remount by the
  caller.
- **The engine's sweep is a loop.** An arrival needs the same two layers played once, armed by arrival
  rather than paused by `useAmbientPause`.
- **An inset box-shadow paints under an element's children.** On a media tile the child is the image,
  so the bright edge has to ride a pseudo-element above it; the board's paste does.
- **The engine's resting ring is not under its travelling ring.** `[data-glw-edge-rest]` is a child of
  the host inset by 20 pixels times `--glw-scale`, while the travelling ring's box is the host's own
  edge (measured at scale 2 on a 400 by 300 host: the ring at 0, 0, the rest ring 40 pixels inside it).
