# The floating layer: three directions, and the contract each one writes

> **ROLE:** the `floating-surfaces` exploration's proposal after four rounds (the review wave,
> 2026-09-14 to 15), kept here so it outlives the track manifest. **NOT LAW** until Will rules; the
> board is `/design/c/floating-surfaces` while it stands, and every paste below is generated by the
> board's own code (`directions.ts`, `candidates.ts`), never typed by hand. The record of each round is
> the CHANGELOG's review-wave entry.
>
> **BELONGS HERE:** what the floating layer should become, as directions with their contracts, plus the
> three knobs that answer today's primitives if no direction is ruled. **NOT HERE:** the board's build
> notes or the findings against the primitives (the track manifest carries those).

Rounds one to three asked what today's floating layer should be **tuned** to and answered it: three
radius rungs, two entrances, one shadow, each generated as real CSS against the primitives' own
data-slots. Will's review (2026-09-15) moved the question:

> "Overall, I don't really love our app design in general, dropdown/nested menus included. The app is
> functionally great, but UI design lags far behind the design work we've been doing for the marketing
> site... I prefer to use this track to explore new dropdown menu designs and variants of our existing
> floating surfaces, rather than nailing the exact details of the current options."

So round four asks the ground-up question (bible 22): if this product had no menus, no popovers and no
sheets, what would the floating layer be, for a host on a laptop and a guest on a phone?

---

## The asks, one word each

1. **The direction:** today, card, glass or command. *This board says card.*
2. **The submenu:** keep it, or delete it and let the three values be a group. *This board says delete.*
3. **The radius:** sharp, nested or round. *This board says nested, which is what card carries.*
4. **The entrance:** one clock or by frequency, and which rule gives way. *This board says by frequency,
   and that rule 15 means one entrance LANGUAGE with rule 12 setting the clock inside it.*
5. **The light in dark:** today or the shadow. *This board says whatever the light board is ruled, since
   the numbers are the same family to the byte.*

---

## What a direction is

Two halves, and the board never blurs them:

- **The material, the radius and the motion are CSS** over the primitives' own data-slots, generated in
  three scopes (one frame, one panel, the whole site). A direction is therefore a paste: "Apply to the
  site" puts it on the real marketing header nav, the real dashboard menus, the guest album and the
  guest's entry drawer.
- **The anatomy and the model are components.** No CSS block adds a header rail, an icon rail or a
  search field, and none can delete a submenu. A ruling for card or command is a change to
  `src/components/ui/dropdown-menu.tsx` that the wiring round lands; `menus.tsx` in the lab is the shape
  of that change, standing up on the real primitives.

Every value is a derivation of the RAW tokens (`--radius`, `--radius-float`, `--radius-action`), never a
number, so the rounding round's retune carries into a direction by itself. A DERIVED token
(`--radius-md` and the rest of the `sm..4xl` scale) is compiled into its utilities by `@theme inline`
and is empty at runtime, and an empty var inside a `calc()` invalidates the declaration silently.

## Direction 1: CARD, the object (recommended)

**A floating surface is a small made object.** It has a title, its groups are labelled, its rows sit on
an icon rail with their state on the right, and the action that cannot be undone sits under a rule of
its own.

| line | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Anatomy | A header row with the subject of the menu; sections with quiet labels; a 20px icon rail; a trailing column for state or a shortcut; a footer rail on its own ground for the destructive row. |
| Material | Opaque popover, the hairline ring kept, the light board's float shadow in light AND dark, so the object has weight on a photograph. |
| Radius | `--flt-r: var(--radius-float)` (8), `--flt-r-item: calc(var(--radius-float) - 4px)` (4), `--flt-r-lg: calc(var(--radius-float) * 2)` (16). This IS the nested rung. |
| Motion | 160ms in, 110ms out: a fade plus a 4px travel toward the trigger plus a hair of scale, on `--ease-emphasis`. The edge family keeps a slide, 260/180 on `--ease-drawer`. |

**Why it wins.** It fixes the thing the review names. Today's menus are anonymous lists: no subject, no
groups, no separation between "Download everything" and "Delete the event". Card gives every floating
surface a title, sections, a rail and a footer, on every surface at once.

**The cost.** The most markup of the three: a two-row overflow menu is suddenly furniture. It is the
right answer for the event menu and the wrong one for a three-row popover, so the anatomy has to be
optional in the primitive rather than mandatory.

## Direction 2: GLASS, the room

**The album's colour is the product, so the floating layer should let it through.** One translucent pane
of the room, lit along its top edge, with no boxes inside it at all.

| line | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Material | The popover surface at 74 percent in dark and 80 in light over an 18px backdrop blur; a hairline of light along the top edge; a border mixed from the foreground rather than a ring (a ring AND a border on a translucent pane draw two edges a pixel apart). |
| Anatomy | By subtraction: no header, no separators that draw, no boxes. A section is a quiet label and a gap; the lit row is a full-bleed wash rather than a chip. |
| Radius | The action family: `--flt-r: var(--radius-action)` (16), rows at 10, big boxes at 28. A pane of light has no corner of its own, so it takes the roundest family in the system. |
| Motion | It condenses: 200ms in from a 5px blur and 0.96 scale, 120ms out, so the panel resolves out of the room instead of popping over it. |

**The cost, three of them, all measured rather than argued.** A backdrop blur is a compositing layer per
open panel. Over a flat app ground it buys nothing at all, so on app-dark and app-light this direction
is a slightly rounder panel, which row 6 of the board shows side by side. And every transparency is a
contrast risk: the mix had to come up twice before a quiet label survived a busy photograph, and the
tooltip had to stop being inverted or its dark text sat on a dark pane.

**Note for rule 15.** If glass is worth it over the album and not over a flat app surface, that is an
argument for a material that varies by GROUND, which rule 15 as written does not allow.

## Direction 3: COMMAND, the model

**A host's menu is a search problem, not a tree.** One surface with a field at the top, grouped rows
underneath, keyboard first, and no nested menu anywhere in the product.

| line | what it is |
| --- | --- |
| Model | The submenu is deleted. Everything a nested menu held is flattened into one list under its own group label, and typing two letters is how you reach it. |
| Anatomy | The field IS the header; rows carry a leading icon and a trailing group hint; a hint rail at the foot says what the keys do, using the product's own `Kbd`. |
| On a phone | The same component is a bottom sheet with the field pinned at the top and big rows, so the model does not need a keyboard to work. |
| Radius | 12px panel, 8px rows, 20px big boxes: the widest gap between panel and row of the three, so a dense list still reads as rows. |
| Motion | 90ms in, 70ms out, a fade and a 4px travel with no scale, because this is the surface a host opens most. |

**Why it does not win here.** Discoverability moves from the tree to the field: a host who never types
sees a shorter list than a nested menu would have shown them. This product's hosts run one event, not
fifty, and a host opens the event menu a dozen times in their life. It is also a HOST answer and not a
marketing one: the header's mega-menu wearing a search field is over-built, which the board shows
rather than hides.

**The one idea to take from it.** Delete the submenu anyway. "Who can upload" is the only nested menu in
the product and it exists to hold three mutually exclusive values behind a hover and a wait. Three
exclusive values are a group, not a tree: carry them inline under their own label inside card's panel.
That is command's argument without command's field, and it also makes a shipped bug moot (a nested
submenu is invisible in the product today; the track manifest has the measurement).

## Today's primitives: the three knobs, if no direction is ruled

Rounds one to three's answer stands whichever way the direction goes, and a direction that wins carries
the radius line with it.

1. **A nested radius, from two tokens.** The panel takes `--radius-float`, its rows take
   `--radius-float-item`, and the two are exactly the row's padding apart so the lit row's corner sits
   concentric inside the panel's (bible 9). A large box takes `--radius-float-lg`.
   `<ruled: sharp 5.6 / 1.6 / 11 | nested 8 / 4 / 16 | round 12 / 8 / 24>`
2. **An origin-AWARE `transform-origin`**, from the primitive's own radix var, never a centre.
3. **A fade beside whatever else moves** (`fade-in-0` / `fade-out-0`): a scale or a travel with no fade
   pops.
4. **One entrance on `--ease-emphasis`, exits faster than enters.**
   `<ruled: one clock 175/120 | by frequency 90/70 high-frequency and 220/150 occasional>`
   The edge family (sheet, drawer) keeps a slide from its own edge on `--ease-drawer`; a sheet that
   zooms is a different component, so an entrance rule addresses the ANCHORED family only and cannot
   beat the edge rule by specificity. The guest entry drawer is in neither: vaul drives its own
   transform for the drag, and an animation on top of that fights the gesture.
5. **One light, by ground.** In light, the float family at 0.09 / 0.13. In dark,
   `<ruled: today, nothing casts and the ring draws the edge | the light board's float family at 0.5 / 0.62>`.
   Whichever is ruled, the values are declared on the ground CLASSES so the nearest ground ancestor wins
   by inheritance: `html` carries `dark` from next-themes while a marketing section carries
   `.surface-paper`, and a panel takes the ground it sits on, not the ground of the document.

Miss any of the five and the surface reads wrong in a way that is hard to name: `rounded-lg` resolves to
the 2px SHARP general-UI radius, a raw `shadow` draws in dark against the elevation contract, a centre
origin detaches the panel from its trigger, and a scale with no fade pops.
