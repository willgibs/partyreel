## The recommendation

**Ember.** A warm dark room, a true grey page, and flare (hue 330) as the accent, with the set-apart panel
given a ground of its own.

A warm cast does real work on a dark ground, where skin has to read against a room and a room should not
look like a dead screen, and it is a tax on paper, where every white dress and every document pays it. So
warm the room and leave the page a true grey. Flare is the one hue left on the wheel that is nowhere near a
state colour and is ours alone. If zero chroma is the brand point exactly as `globals.css` records it,
**Ladder** is Ember with the warmth taken out and nothing else on the board changes.

The paste the ruling lands is generated on the board's last section from whatever the dock is claiming, so
it is never typed here: pick a palette, open "The ruling, as a paste", copy the block. It covers
`:root, .surface-paper`, the new `.surface-mat`, `.dark`, `.surface-ink`, the media well, and either the
cinema override in `marketing.css` or the instruction to delete it.

## Round six, the clarity round: the board became a catalog

Will read round five and said what it had become: "it almost feels like I'm reading a PhD on color theory.
We're simply exploring new color palettes, having a dozen polished variants with preview palettes with some
demo UI to config & compare would've been far more helpful than this massive mountain we've created. Then I
end up with six configs that aren't clearly explained."

He was right about the shape. Five rounds had built a machine rather than a choice: six dark sets, five
light ones, four accents and five switches, which is hundreds of reachable states and an answer in none of
them. The colour work stands and none of it was thrown away. What round six added is the one thing the
machine could not: somebody chose. **Twelve of those states are now finished palettes with names**, each a
whole answer (a dark room, a dark slab, a page, a set-apart panel, a media well, all the text greys, one
accent), and the rest of the argument is collapsed underneath them.

The twelve, in the order they appear:

| Palette | What it is | Why it is on the list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Today** | The site exactly as it ships | The one to come back to; a ruling of Today changes no line |
| **Ember** (recommended) | A warm dark room, a true grey page, flare | Warm the room, leave the page alone |
| **Ladder** | A neutral dark in three steps, a true grey page, flare | Ember at chroma zero, for keeping the zero-chroma decision |
| **Slate** | A cold dark room, a daylight page, the save blue | A cool ground makes a warm photograph read warmer; no new hue |
| **Gallery** | A neutral dark, a daylight page on a dead grey mat, no accent | The purist reading kept whole: only photographs are coloured |
| **Studio** | One derived dark room, a page where the card IS the paper, no accent | One number tunes the dark side; leans entirely on depth |
| **Loft** | A dark that is never black, the card is the paper, the reel's violet | One dark register instead of two; loses the true-black economy |
| **Press** | Warm on both sides, with flare | One temperature through the product, so nothing flips at the seam |
| **Reel** | A cold dark room, a true grey page, the reel's violet | The accent and the signature moment become one hue |
| **Signal** | One derived dark room, a true grey page, the save blue | The answer with no new hue to hold anywhere |
| **Daylight** | A warm dark room against a daylight page, flare | Deliberately cross-cast, the way a warm print hangs on a cool wall |
| **Dusk** | A warm dark room, today's page untouched, flare | The smallest change that still moves anything |

A palette earns a row only if a reviewer could prefer it for a reason he could say out loud. Two that
differ only in a number are one row, which is why there are twelve and not thirty; fewer than eight is not
a catalog and more than sixteen is a wall, and `registers.test.ts` holds that range. Between them the
twelve wear every dark set and every light set the board carries, so the catalog is a span rather than
four rows of the same argument.

**The board that carries it.** Thirteen sections became six, eight asks became four and seven switches
became one. Each card shows every ground the palette declares as a strip (the well, the room, the slab,
then the panel and the page, deepest to brightest), the text steps drawn on the two grounds type lands on,
the accent and the six state colours in both modes, and the same piece of the product built from the
production Card, Button, Input, Badge and DropdownMenu at true pixels, dark beside light. Twelve of those
fit four across at the review width. Picking a card is picking the palette everywhere below it: the
surface-ladder wipe against today, the three remaining calls, the real routes loaded beside today, the app
compositions and the paste all read one dock control.

**The three calls that survive a pick**, because none of them is a colour: how far the accent reaches
(which call sites read the token), whether a dark card is solid or see-through (every palette changes it by
accident, so it should be a ruling rather than a side effect), and whether a third, fainter text colour
exists at all (37 places fade the second grey by hand today, 19 of them at exactly the same fade).

**What left the board, and where it went.** The oklab rulers, the two set tables and the register diagram
are gone as canvases and are collapsed argument paragraphs on the catalog section: light mode's five
surfaces inside 0.037, the dark panel that ships lighter than the card it sits in, the empty 0.450 to 0.905
middle, and why cinema and ink are two grounds of one mode rather than two darks. An argument earns a
canvas while it is the thing being decided; once it is settled evidence it earns a paragraph.

## Round five: onto the lab kit

The board moved onto the lab kit's template, which changed what a reviewer meets first rather than what the
board argues. Three pieces of it got better on the way. The live sections became live PAGES: the four
production sections this board used to portal into an iframe of its own making retired to the kit's Frame
loading the real routes, today on the left and the candidate on the right, scrolled together and wearing
the exact block the Apply button hands the site, so the footer seam and the accent on the mark are judged
on the page rather than on a drawing of it. The today-beside-the-candidate frame became a wipe, one canvas
with the seam on a slider, so a 0.02 step is compared across four pixels instead of across a gap. And the
two set cards became two tables where the row IS the dock control. No set, number or recommendation
changed.

## Round four: the model

Round four stopped adding candidates and named the system instead. Cinema and ink turned out to be one
mode's two grounds rather than two darks: the room a dark chapter sits in, and the slab a dark leaf makes
on a light page. Light has the same pair (the paper, and the mat, which is the `/contact` panel nobody had
named), and the media well belongs to neither mode because it is always dark. Four registers and a bed,
where the product had five unnamed grounds and a literal. The ruling then split in two, as Will asked: six
dark sets beside five light ones, thirty pairs, and three new darks (a warm room, a cool one, and one
lifted off black that argues dark needs a single register) beside two new papers. The split immediately
produced an answer no earlier round could have reached, because warming had only ever been askable about
both modes at once: warm the room, leave the page a true grey. Lab only, no production byte.
