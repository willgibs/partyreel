> clarity round, 2026-09-15, into the cool round and the stepped review, 2026-09-16), kept here so it
> outlives the track manifest.
> **NOT LAW** until Will rules; the board is `/design/lab/palette` while it stands, and every paste it
> offers is generated by the board's own code, never typed by hand. The record of each round is the
> CHANGELOG's review-wave entry.
>

## The recommendation

**Graphite.** A very black room at 0.105, a very white page at 0.995, every surface between them one of
Apple's cool greys, and no accent worn until the switch says otherwise.

Will's note is about grounds rather than about greys: the black and the white carry the contrast, and
everything sitting on them is cool rather than merely lighter. Graphite is the middle of the cool family,
deep enough at 0.105 to be a ground and tinted at half again Apple's amount so it reads cool on a black
room. **Onyx** is the same answer with the cool halved, for anyone who wants the direction without the
colour; **Steel** is it doubled. If the page being a grey rather than a white is acceptable, **Mist** fixes
the light side outright: a pure white card lifts 0.037 off it instead of 0.007.

The paste the ruling lands is generated on the board's last section from whatever the dock is claiming, so
it is never typed here: pick a palette, open "The ruling, as a paste", copy the block. It covers
`:root, .surface-paper`, the new `.surface-mat`, `.dark`, `.surface-ink`, the media well, either the cinema
override in `marketing.css` or the instruction to delete it, and the accent block only while the accent
switch is on.

## Round eight, the stepped review: one pick, then one question per specimen

No palette changed. What changed is how the board is met. The twelve are variants of ONE thing, so they are
decided by one pick (`catalog.mode: "pick-one"`, the `palette` ask, whose options are the twelve card ids
plus **None of these**, which clears the board and carries the new directions in its note), and the real
product sits under the tiles wearing whatever is pressed. Every card and every ask now says what it lands
as, in files, because a pick-one catalog is not ruled card by card and the card is where "what would this
change" has to be readable.

The four calls that survive a pick became steps of their own, each drawing every option on ONE specimen:
the accent and its reach on a dashboard carrying all three of the accent's jobs at once, the dark card on
one card over a photograph with its resolved `--card` printed beside it, the third text step on one block
across the three grounds type lands on. The reach is **staged** behind `accent=own`: it is meaningless
until an accent is on, so it stays off the desk until then and is moot the moment the accent is ruled off.

Two shapes retired with it. The accent WALL (six numbered job rows, each drawing none beside the palette's
hue, 1,740 pixels tall) existed because the board could only show one state at a time; the tiles make the
comparison, so the specimen only has to be one honest screen. And the app section folded into the real
pages: one Screen toggle now carries the four marketing routes, the demo album's own public route and the
two signed-in screens, all wearing the pick, which is what the stage under a pick should be.

## Round seven, the cool round: the catalog leaned cool, and the accent became a config

Will asked for this a few questions into his first sitting on the round-six catalog, before ruling on any
of its cards. Verbatim: "I'm a much bigger fan of the cooler gray direction in slate, studio, and reel,
think they feel more modern, clean, and combat less harshly with a very media-forward dashboard. Looks
beautiful with the very black/white backgrounds for solid contrast then cooler surfaces rather than darker
bland grays. Many of the warmer tones feel like they'd clash with a colorful mix of photos. Slate could
even be less blue, but I'd like more 'cool' gray options. Apple has a beautiful palette, but we wouldn't
use that blue they use." Plus: one optional accent per palette, as a config.

**The measurement the round turns on.** Converted to oklch, Apple's six system greys sit at **hue 286**
with an almost constant chroma of 0.0066, and their blue sits at **257**. Rounds three to six built this
board's cool cast at **258**, which is the hue of their blue and not of their greys, on a chroma curve that
rose with lightness the way the warm table does. Both were wrong for a grey: a cool grey's tint is a
constant property of the family, not something the light does to it. The band is now theirs, `gain` is how
far past or short of their amount a set sits, and that single number is most of what made Slate read blue.

| Measured reference | oklch |
| --- | --- |
| `#1C1C1E` gray6 (dark) | 0.227 c 0.0038 h 286 |
| `#2C2C2E` gray5 (dark) | 0.294 c 0.0036 h 286 |
| `#3A3A3C` gray4 (dark) | 0.349 c 0.0034 h 286 |
| `#636366` gray2 (dark) | 0.501 c 0.0047 h 286 |
| `#8E8E93` systemGray | 0.648 c 0.0073 h 286 |
| `#E5E5EA` gray5 (light) | 0.923 c 0.0067 h 286 |
| `#F2F2F7` gray6 (light) | 0.963 c 0.0066 h 286 |
| `#3C3C43` secondary label | 0.359 c 0.0120 h 286 |
| `#007AFF` systemBlue | 0.603 c 0.2177 h **257** |

**Four new dark sets, built from that sentence.** A very black ground carries the contrast; the surfaces
above it are cool greys rather than slightly lighter dead ones. `Onyx` (room 0.075, half Apple's tint),
`Graphite` (0.105, one and a half), `Steel` (0.125, two) and `Pitch` (0.030, their own ladder above true
black) are one family at four distances from black. On the light side, `Pearl` is a 0.995 page with their
grey ladder underneath it, and `Mist` is the grouped inversion: their gray6 as the page, with a pure white
card on it. The warm light set and the lifted dark set left the board with Press, Daylight and Loft.

**The ink carries the tint too**, which breaks round three's own rule that a surface carries the cast and
ink does not. That rule is right on the warm side, where a tinted black yellows. Apple's secondary label is
tinted harder than any of their greys, and on a cool page a neutral ink is the one thing in the frame that
disagrees. The cool light sets run their text steps at the same 286 at a third of the chroma.

**The accent is a config.** Every palette declares the one hue that would pair with its grey; a page-wide
switch, **off by default**, decides whether it is worn, and it flips all twelve cards, the accent specimen
and the real pages at once. The token does three unrelated jobs, which is why the reach is its own
question: **identity** (the mark), **attention** (the primary action, the focus ring, the badge, the wizard
step, the live dot) and the **stand-in** for a photograph nobody has taken (the frames family). Off resolves to the alias that ships, so the paste prints no accent block at all. One new
hue joined for the cool family: **Teal 200**, the widest gap left on the wheel (50 degrees from `--save`,
50 from `--success`, 86 from the 286 the greys are tinted with, and nowhere near the blue that was ruled
out). The config retired two cards outright: with the accent off by default, "the palette with no accent"
is every palette, so Gallery and Signal were arguing about a hue rather than about a grey.

The twelve, in the order they appear:

| Palette | What it is | Declares | Board's call |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Today** | The site exactly as it ships | Blue | kill |
| **Ladder** | The same rhythm at chroma zero | Flare | kill |
| **Ember** | A warm dark room, a true grey page | Flare | kill |
| **Onyx** | The blackest room, half Apple's tint, a very white page | Teal | refine |
| **Graphite** (recommended) | A very black ground, a very white page, their greys between | Teal | **ship** |
| **Steel** | The cool at twice Apple's tint, on the same white page | Flare | refine |
| **Pitch** | A true black room with their own grey steps, on their grey page | Teal | refine |

Three of the twelve are controls rather than proposals: Today changes no line, Ladder is the same rhythm at
chroma zero (put it on A and any cool card on B and the question "is the cool doing anything" answers
itself in one gesture), and Ember is the one warm card kept so the reversal is visible rather than
asserted. Every card now lays a **colourful mix of five photographs** on its own well, spanning gold,
pastel, night blue, laser and a warm stage, because that mix is exactly what the warm tones were said to
clash with. The two hard cases (a white dress on a white wall, and one candle) are still asked for from the
media kit.

## Round six, the clarity round: the board became a catalog

Will read round five and said what it had become: "it almost feels like I'm reading a PhD on color theory.
We're simply exploring new color palettes, having a dozen polished variants with preview palettes with some
demo UI to config & compare would've been far more helpful than this massive mountain we've created."

He was right about the shape. Five rounds had built a machine rather than a choice: six dark sets, five
light ones, four accents and five switches, which is hundreds of reachable states and an answer in none of
them. What round six added is the one thing the machine could not: somebody chose. Twelve of those states
became finished palettes with names, each a whole answer (a dark room, a dark slab, a page, a set-apart
panel, a media well, all the text greys, one accent), and the rest of the argument is collapsed underneath
them. Thirteen sections became six, eight asks became four, seven switches became one.

A palette earns a row only if a reviewer could prefer it for a reason he could say out loud. Two that
differ only in a number are one row, and since round seven two that differ only in an accent are one row as
well; fewer than eight is not a catalog and more than sixteen is a wall, and `registers.test.ts` holds that
range. Between them the twelve wear every dark set and every light set the board carries, so the catalog is
a span rather than four rows of the same argument.

**The calls that survive a pick**, because none of them is a colour a card decides: whether the site
carries an accent at all, how far it reaches if it does (which call sites read the token), whether a dark
card is solid or see-through (every palette changes it by accident, so it should be a ruling rather than a
side effect), and whether a third, fainter text colour exists at all (37 places fade the second grey by
hand today, 19 of them at exactly the same fade). Round eight made each of them a step of its own.

## The model, from round four, which every palette still answers

Cinema and ink are one mode's two grounds rather than two darks: the **room** a dark chapter sits in, and
the **slab** a dark leaf makes on a light page, which has to sit lighter than the room because a room
dropped into paper reads as a hole. Light has the same pair (the **paper**, and the **mat**, which is the
`/contact` panel nobody had named), and the media **well** belongs to neither mode because it is always
dark. Four registers and a bed, where the product ships five unnamed grounds plus a literal black in the
lightbox. The dark half and the light half are ruled separately, which is what let round four name a pair
no earlier round could reach, and what lets round seven put a very black room under a very white page.

The earlier rounds (the oklab rulers, the register diagram, the two set tables, the move onto the lab kit
and the live routes) are in git and in the board's own `history`.
