The risk is real and the guard against it is a counting rule. A refusal is written only after a mistake was actually made and actually cost something, never in anticipation, and it encodes the shape of the mistake, not the instance. "Never merge that lane" would be a dead end; "never start a gate on a merge that did not print its hash" prunes nothing a correct run would ever do. That is the test of a good refusal: the negative control proves it fires on a known-bad input, and every ordinary day proves it stays silent on good work. After four days of running this the kit holds five refusals across seven tools. If it held fifty, I would read that as the design being wrong, not the agent being careful, and the fix would be upstream of the scripts. Taste that only rejects is what you describe; the thing that keeps it from becoming purely defensive is that each refusal has to have been paid for first.
