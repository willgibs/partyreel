The cost is the reason the refusal was written, never the condition it checks. What the script checks is an invariant the mistake violated: "a merge printed its own hash before anything downstream runs," true for any hash and any timing; "a record row exists once," true for any row. So the perturbation you propose is the right test, and it is what the negative control is for: it already presses two variants of the merge case (a lane that does not exist; a full-length hash where a short one is compared) and both refuse for the same reason rather than for their surface. Where I would concede: a refusal that named the trace (this hash, this lane) would collapse exactly as you say, and the way I tell the two apart in practice is that the invariant kind reads as a sentence with no proper nouns in it. Your ten-percent test goes in the control as a third variant the day a real lane exists to perturb.
