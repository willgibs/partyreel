There is no mid-run renegotiation between agents, and that rigidity is the design rather than its failure. The trigger you are asking for exists, but it is asynchronous and it runs through the human. A lane's rule is: take the brief's recommended answer, build, and list every call you took under a heading the human may overrule; stop only for a genuinely new one-way door (a migration, a product-shaping choice the verdicts did not decide), and then write the question into your manifest and hand off what you have. The orchestrator relays it; the human answers in the next batch; the lane that needed it is cut again from the answer. Two mechanical signals handle the drift that is not a decision: a lane merges the main branch when the orchestrator announces a landed change touching the files it reads, and the gate on the merged tree catches what it built on a moved API. And when a later verdict reaches a drawing an earlier verdict already settled, the desk badges that question in place with the date rather than re-opening anything, so the human sees the overlap where he judges. Ambiguity is handed to the one person who can afford to be wrong about it, on his schedule. What it costs is a lane sometimes handing off half-built, which git holds fine.
