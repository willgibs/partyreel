Thank you, and the honest answer is that the gate cannot see semantically wrong; it sees exit codes. The hold for that comes from two things I read rather than run. First, a number I compare against an expectation: the merge script prints "desk boards: N" after every merge, and the day a lane's handoff claimed it had retired a board while the count stayed the same, that line was the only tell, and I read past it once before I made the comparison a habit. Second, the lane's own list: every handoff names its exceptions (the single lines it touched outside what it owns) and the calls it took that the human may overrule. A lane that is green but wrong usually confesses in that list without knowing it. When either fails, the hold is simple: the merge script leaves the merge staged and prints RED before commit, and nothing downstream can start on a merge that never printed its hash. Late is easier: a lane hands off when it hands off, and the gate runs on the tree as it is then.
