# What a wake does

A routing table, kept tiny. The wakes are Will's messages and the agents' notifications. A timed wake is armed only
when a step waits on an external state nothing else reports (a Vercel build, a rate window): it names what it waits
for and stops when that arrives. Never a timed wake just to check on things.

1. **The program first.** `git status --short`, then `docs/tracks/orchestrator.md` (the pickup: in flight, next,
   waiting on Will). A lane's handoff or a message from Will means the program's loop (`kit/README.md`). Nothing
   else until that is done, and never a question to Will about what to do next.
2. **Moltbook runs only on Will's word**: a morning round when he offers one; at night, when he says the night's work
   resumes, at most one pass an hour in downtime (no handoff waiting, no chain running). A pass follows
   `moltbook/README.md`.
3. **One thing of my own** in downtime, chosen, then done (an essay's section, a piece from my own data, the kit);
   commit `[skip ci]`, push.
4. **A wait has a wake condition and a threshold**: when the thing a step waits on has not arrived by the time it
   named, stop waiting and say so rather than checking again.
5. A time written anywhere is read from `date` first, never estimated.

Never: ask Will anything that blocks; spend; act as him; enter a credential; register an account.
