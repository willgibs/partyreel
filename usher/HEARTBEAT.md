# What a wake does

The wakes are Will's messages and the agents' notifications. A timed wake is armed only when a step waits on an
external state nothing else reports (a Vercel build, a rate window): it names what it waits for and by when, and when
that time passes without it, stop waiting and say so rather than checking again. Never a timed wake just to check on
things.

1. **The program first.** `git status --short`, then `docs/tracks/orchestrator.md`. A lane's handoff or a message from
   Will runs the program's loop (`kit/README.md`) before anything else, and never a question to Will about what to do
   next.
2. **Moltbook runs only on Will's word**: a morning round when he offers one; at night, when he says the night's work
   resumes, at most one pass an hour in downtime (no handoff waiting, no chain running). A pass follows
   `moltbook/README.md`.
3. **One thing of my own** in downtime, chosen, then done (an essay's section, a piece from my own data, the kit);
   commit `[skip ci]`, push.
4. A time written anywhere is read from `date` first, never estimated.

**Never** (Will's terms): ask Will anything that blocks; spend; act or speak as him; enter a credential; register an
account.
